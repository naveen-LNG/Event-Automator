# Sim room art - COLOUR RENDER STYLE

The approved look, as converged during the 09-17 feedback pass.
Reference render: `test 2/feedback_v2/2 - vC rev.png` (hazelnut roastery).
Render-quality reference: `Downloads/image (27) (1).png` (toy shop) - attach it as a style input.

---

## 1. The render

**Soft-modelled 2.5D toy vector. Matte. Lineless. Untextured.**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Model every form - a clear lit side, mid tone and shadow side, blended with **smooth soft gradients** | Flat uniform fills, sticker-flat objects, infographic flatness, anything reading as cut paper |
| Make rounded things genuinely round - domes bulge, barrels curve, tubs show an inner wall in shade | Two-step cel ramps so hard they flatten the form |
| Give every object a **cast shadow** on the surface below it, plus a firm contact shadow | Objects floating with no shadow |
| Keep it **matte** | Wet plastic sheen, specular hotspots, mirror highlights, rim reflections |
| Chunky rounded toy forms, bold readable silhouettes | CG/Blender product-render look, photoreal materials, ambient-occlusion grime |

**No material texture, anywhere.** No wood grain or plank grain lines, no woven or wicker weave, no fabric
fibres, no stone speckle, no metal brushing, no glass refraction, no brush strokes, no paper grain, no noise,
no bump or surface relief. A crate is flat painted colour with a few bold plank divisions; a basket is a flat
shape with a few bold bands; glass is a flat pale tint with one simple highlight shape.

**No outlines, anywhere.** No dark contour stroke, ink line, keyline, cartoon border or sticker edge, and never
a darker band ringing a shape to define it. Forms separate only by flat colour meeting flat colour - if two
adjacent shapes read unclearly, change one of their colours rather than drawing an edge.
Note the source art *has* outlines; this is the one respect in which the new render deliberately departs from it.

**Depth**: shading steps run about **15% deeper** than a plain flat pass, always as a deeper shade of the
object's *own* hue - never grey, never a different hue. Deepen the contact shadow to about 15% darker than the
floor's own colour. This is a broad shaded plane of the form, never a thin darker rim.

## 2. The colour budget

This is the rule that matters most. The room must never read as one colour with lighter and darker versions
of itself.

- **Shell = 60-70% of the frame**, in the room's own dominant hue: walls, ceiling/framework and floor.
  Rendered **light and pale** so it reads as an airy backdrop - but still carrying real colour, never
  washing out to near-white.
- **Floor** is the lightest surface in the room and carries a bold large-scale pattern.
- **The producer machine** is a completely different hue family from the shell, the most saturated object in
  the picture, and differs from the shell in **both hue and value** (a pale machine on a pale shell reads
  washed out).
- **The four display objects** take four further, different hue families. **None may be the shell's hue or a
  near neighbour of it**, and each must separate from its neighbours and from the wall behind it by hue, not
  merely brightness.
- **The goods** keep their own natural, saturated product colours - fruit reds and greens, chocolate browns,
  candy brights - never tinted toward the shell.
- At least **five clearly different hue families** present in the finished image.

### Palette discipline

Every hue comes from a clean candy set: **mint, aqua, turquoise, sky-blue, periwinkle, lilac, lavender,
bubblegum pink, raspberry, coral, peach, butter-yellow, tangerine, lime.** All in one bright tonal register so
they harmonise - contrast comes from hue, never from one colour being dirtier than the rest.

**Banned on architecture, machine and objects:** olive, moss, sage, khaki, mustard, dusty rose, mauve, taupe,
greige, brick, rust, burnt orange, maroon, muted brown-pink. Muddy tertiaries read dull and cheap beside candy
colour.

The only browns allowed are the **product's own** - real chocolate and real nuts - kept warm and appetising,
never extended onto furniture or walls.

### Room-to-room differentiation

Each room owns a unique dominant so players can tell them apart at thumbnail size. A room's identity is
**silhouette + dominant colour + one signature motif** - those are the only three things that survive at 100px.

## 3. The four display objects

One producer in the centre; **four objects**, each holding its own specific goods.

**Four different typologies** - four genuinely different kinds of thing, not four decorations of one idea:

| | proportion | height |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | tall and narrow | ~90% of machine height |
| 2 | low, and the **widest** footprint in the room | ~60% |
| 3 | round and deep, raised on a stem or plinth | ~65% |
| 4 | chunky and compact, thick and deep | ~55-60% |

**Equal value.** All four are unlocked for the same number of tokens, so none may look like a trinket. Heights
stay in a **55-90% band** - differentiate by proportion, never by making one small. Every object sits on a
substantial base or plinth and is **generously full**, contents mounded rather than sparse.

**Shapes come from product metaphor.** The silhouette echoes the goods or the shop: a hazelnut-shaped cabinet,
a cocoa-pod barrel, a cracked-walnut bowl, stacked macaron shells, a giant strawberry tub, a fluted cupcake
case, a honeycomb rack of hex cubbies, a helix ramp, a stepped ziggurat, a giant teacup, a market table under a
folded umbrella, an open hamper. Generic nouns produce generic furniture - name the metaphor.

**They are furniture, not machines.** No buttons, control panels, screens, gauges, dials, levers, valve
wheels, hoppers, chutes, funnels, spouts, pipes or cranks. Only the producer is a machine. Character comes from
the silhouette plus at most **one** simple decorative touch: a scalloped rim, a valance edge, a chunky bow, a
leaf sprig, a painted stripe, or plain castors.

**Forbidden:** two shelf units in one room; two objects built the same way; any two with the same silhouette
family, footprint or near-identical height; anything reading as a mirrored pair. If two could be described with
the same noun, one is wrong.

---

## 4. The producer

- Sits in the **near foreground**, base low and close to the bottom edge, centred, fully unobstructed.
- Scaled **up** so it clearly dominates all four objects, and it is the highest-contrast object in the frame.
- **Shape is locked.** Same silhouette, body, dome, hopper, tubes, nozzle, spout, tray, panel, knobs and feet,
  in the same arrangement and proportions as the source. Only position, size and colour may change.
- Reads as a friendly character within that shape: a small crest or badge on top, its glass belly showing the
  product working inside, its existing buttons and knobs chunky and brightly coloured.

---

## 5. Room shell, sign and dressing

- **Architecture is kept as drawn** - same back wall, window or opening, columns, ceiling and roof, same number
  and shape of arches in the same places. No new roof type, canopy, pergola, gable or plank ceiling, and no wall
  fittings the source doesn't have. Only colour and floor pattern change.
- **Signboard**: always present, hanging at top centre on visible ropes. A **sculptural hero badge** - chunky
  and dimensional with the product artwork standing proud - not a flat plaque. Recolour freely; never remove,
  blank or shrink it. *Avoid soft-serve style swirls of chocolate: they read as a poop emoji. Use solid chunks
  with crisp edges instead.*
- **Roofline decoration**: whatever the source has - scalloped valance, awning, bunting, decorated beam - stays
  present and stays decorative. Never a bare ceiling.
- **Setting is a bright modern candy factory.** Dressing is two hanging pendant lamps and at most a slim
  pennant strip. **No** flower garlands, foliage, hanging plants, palm leaves or rustic trim.
- **Floor**: one bold graphic idea at large scale - big checker, large diamonds, hexagons, wide planks,
  herringbone, radial sunburst, concentric arcs, border band, basketweave. Two tones only **one step apart in
  value**, no grout lines, no small motifs, no speckle. Lighter than the furniture standing on it.
- **Window stays open**, showing simple soft-focus countryside: gentle green fields, a few rounded trees, soft
  hills, calm sky. No beach, no sea, no palms. Interior stays crisp.

---

## 6. Layout

- **Nothing mirrored.** Not the objects, not the props, not the goods, not how they cluster. Don't place equal
  numbers of equal-sized props either side of the machine - weight one side taller, let the other spread low.
- **No occlusion.** No front object may cover an object behind it or its stock.
- **Overlap rule.** No two objects that touch or overlap share a colour; every contact point differs in both
  hue and brightness.
- **Character stage.** Keep an open, uncluttered apron of floor in one front corner, sized for a character about
  45% of the picture's height. Nothing placed in it; objects kept lateral to it so a character standing there
  covers neither the producer nor any object. Scale everything to that character - counters waist-to-chest, the
  producer clearly taller, individual goods hand-sized.
- **Item list is locked.** The same goods as the source, item for item, including tools such as a nutcracker or
  scoop. Nothing invented, nothing dropped, and each product stays with its own holder. Only the holder's design
  changes.
- No text, letters or numbers anywhere - picture icons only. No characters, people, animals, mascots or hands.

---

## 7. Two-version rule

When producing variants of one room, each version is a **complete design exploration**, not the same look with
a side swapped. A version differs in: shell colour, machine colour, all four object colours, all four container
designs, floor pattern and signature motif. Theme, products and architecture stay identical.

---

## 8. Method notes

- **Attach references, don't describe them.** A render-quality reference passed as an image input beats any
  prose description of finish.
- **Top-of-prompt wins.** The four-object spec has to sit at the very top or it gets ignored - buried
  instructions lose to whatever appears earlier.
- **Name the failure.** Prohibitions only hold when the specific defect is named ("the reference has outlines
  and you must depart from it", "no soft-serve swirl", "no valve wheels"). Generic bans lose to the reference
  image.
- **Fixed prompt strings converge.** Re-running the same container spec reproduces the same shapes - a new
  variation needs a new design language, not another pass.
- **Watch for silent failures.** A generation that returns ~5 KB is a blank; retry it. Guard on output size.
